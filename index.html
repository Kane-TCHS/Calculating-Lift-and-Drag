1

Start with the model

Lift and drag use the same dynamic-pressure terms. The coefficient describes how the wing’s shape and configuration respond to the airflow.

Lift

L = ½ CL A ρ v²

Upward aerodynamic force

Drag

D = ½ CD A ρ v²

Force opposing the aircraft’s motion

Air density

ρ = p ÷ [0.2869 (T + 273.1)]

Use p in kPa and T in °C. This gives ρ in kg/m³.

Think about the squared velocity. If speed doubles while everything else stays the same, lift and drag become four times as large. The equations describe aerodynamic forces; weight is still the gravitational force pulling the aircraft down.

Find the air density

Density changes with pressure and temperature. For altitude-based questions, estimate standard temperature first.

T (°C) = 15 − 0.0065 × altitude (m)

Then use the pressure supplied in the problem:

ρ = p ÷ [0.2869 × (T + 273.1)]

Worked example: minimum lift at takeoff

A Cessna has mass 1,056 kg, wing area 18.2 m², and takeoff speed 102 km/h. Air temperature is 22°C and pressure is 101.3 kPa. Find the minimum lift coefficient at liftoff.

1. Liftoff condition: At the instant the wheels leave the runway, minimum lift is approximately equal to aircraft weight.
2. Convert mass to weight: W = mg. Use that force for L.
3. Convert speed: divide km/h by 3.6 to get m/s.
4. Find density: substitute p and T in the density equation.
5. Rearrange: CL = 2L ÷ (Aρv²).

Weight = (1,056 kg)(9.81 m/s²). Convert 102 km/h to m/s. Density uses 101.3 kPa and 22°C. Substitute weight for L, then solve for CL.

5

Rearrange for drag or its coefficient

The drag equation has the same structure as lift. Choose the form that leaves the unknown by itself.

Find drag

D = ½ CDAρv²

Find drag coefficient

CD = 2D ÷ (Aρv²)

Find speed from lift

v = √[2L ÷ (CLAρ)]

Quick check: A wing has CD = 0.074, area 350 m², density 0.45 kg/m³, and speed 84.3 m/s. What drag force does the equation predict?

Flight problems: takeoff and landing

Work each setup on paper. Enter only the requested final quantity to check your calculation. Round to three significant figures unless you prefer more precision.

Use the provided pressure and temperature or calculate temperature from altitude as directed. Keep units consistent, and show your conversions in your own work. The hint for each problem suggests a first step.
Problem 1 · 777 takeoff from Denver · find minimum CL

Altitude 1,625 m; pressure 101.8 kPa; speed 99.4 m/s; takeoff weight 2,897,229 N; wing area 418.9 m². Find the minimum lift coefficient. Use altitude to estimate temperature.

First find T from altitude, then ρ from pressure and temperature. At liftoff set L equal to the given weight, then rearrange for CL.
Problem 2 · 777 landing at O’Hare · find drag

Altitude 668 m; pressure 98.8 kPa; landing speed 196.4 mi/h; CD = 0.034; wing area 429.1 m². Find drag in newtons. Use altitude to estimate temperature.

Convert mi/h to m/s. Find density, then use the drag equation. The speed must be squared.
Problem 3 · 777 landing at O’Hare · find touchdown speed

Use the O’Hare conditions from Problem 2. The wing has CL = 1.46 and area 4,618.8 ft². Starting mass was 225,982 kg. The flight used 7,534 L of fuel, at 0.781 kg/L. Find touchdown speed in mi/h.

Subtract fuel mass from starting mass, convert the remaining mass to weight, convert area from ft² to m², then solve the lift equation for v. Convert m/s to mi/h at the end.
Problem 4 · 777-200LR takeoff from Dubai · find minimum CL

The aircraft is loaded to 92% of maximum takeoff mass (347,452 kg). Wing area 427.8 m²; speed 376 km/h; temperature 34.5°C; pressure 101.3 kPa. Find the minimum lift coefficient.

Use 92% of maximum mass, then convert to weight. Convert speed to m/s. Use the stated temperature and pressure to find density.
Problem 5 · Extra lift for the Dubai takeoff · find lift margin

Use the same aircraft and conditions as Problem 4, but set CL = 1.24. How much lift is available above the aircraft’s weight at the same takeoff speed?

Calculate lift using CL = 1.24, then subtract the aircraft’s weight. A positive result is the lift margin.
Problem 6 · 777 landing in Hong Kong · find minimum speed

Use the same starting aircraft from Problem 4. Fuel used: 82,900 L at 0.803 kg/L. Landing conditions: 32.8°C and 101.29 kPa. Wing area 427.8 m²; CL = 1.24. Find the minimum landing speed in mi/h.

Start with the loaded mass from Problem 4 and subtract fuel mass. Convert remaining mass to weight, use Hong Kong density, solve for v, and convert m/s to mi/h.
Problem 7 · Hong Kong landing · find drag

Use the landing speed from Problem 6. The same aircraft has CD = 0.087. Find the drag force at landing.

Use speed in m/s in the drag equation. You can reuse the Hong Kong density from Problem 6.
